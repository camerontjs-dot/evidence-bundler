## Declaration and subject rules

Declared means exactly two materially distinct required propositions whose conjunction conserves the root. Preserve modality, negation, quantifiers, numbers/dates, attribution, scope and references; add or omit no obligation. OR flattening, conditional loss, ambiguous/context-dependent roots, duplicate children and non-two-obligation roots are ineligible. No truth, evidence adequacy or source-authority judgment.

SCHEMAS.json fixes primary/checker wire fields. Each child subject is an exact UTF-8 root span. Explicit subjects additionally bind an exact child span. An elliptical child copies a root/parent anchor exactly, with inherited status and no child span. No new/fuzzy entity, evidence-derived alias, automatic subject production or post-retrieval repair. The checker must accept the exact proposal digest and all five checks: structure, distinctness, conservation, subject anchors, inheritance.

After declarations freeze, subjects freeze separately. Retain the original profile: ≥1 inherited child and ≥2 distinct subject anchors of the same entity class per packet. These must arise faithfully. Do not manufacture ellipsis or selectively replace already accepted roots to fit controls. Failure gives `BLOCKED_SUBJECT_CONTROL_POPULATION` before corpus access.

Wrong-subject donors: within the same packet/entity class, sort distinct anchors then child IDs; rotate to the next distinct anchor, wrapping once. Every child needs a plausible donor. Wrong-child mapping swaps the two children within each accepted parent. Freeze exact donor and permutation identities before sources; do not adapt to retrieval.
